Do not copy Hemingway's product photography or imply that its current online stock is Zebra's live inventory. Use original Zebra Gallery photographs for product images. Hemingway's descriptions identify Welega chairs as carved from a single block of wood, with deep patina from use, and identify Wanza (Cordia africana) as the wood on the cited chair pages. Its Jimma chair pages connect the form to household furniture associated with King Abba Jifar and describe sculptural carved openings; listed materials include Wanza wood and paint. Treat these as reference descriptions only and verify wood species, age, condition, and origin for each Zebra item before asserting them.

## Stool research — do not confuse regional origins

Hemingway's broader stools catalogue has many individually listed antique Tonga stools (Zimbabwe), Asante stools (Ghana), and other African seating. These are useful competitors for how to present one-of-a-kind stools, condition, patina, and individual SKU inventory, but they are NOT Ethiopian stools and must not be presented as Zebra's Ethiopian stock.

Useful stool market references:
- Hemingway's general furniture/stools collection lists individual Tonga stools at reference prices from roughly $250 to $700, plus Asante stools at roughly $1,000 to $1,800 and a Baule double-sided stool at $1,500. These are asking prices, not verified sale prices.
- Sotheby's Buy Now listing “Ethiopian Carved Wood Stool” describes a 20th-century wood stool/side table, carved from one block with a concave seat and three legs joined in a geometric design. Dimensions: height 13.5 in; diameter 16 in. Price not displayed.
- Harvard Peabody Museum records a Jimma stool, carved wood, with a concave/indented round seat and three/four-support description. Published dimensions: 35.1 × 27.8 × 26.9 cm; museum field is labelled “Other”, so do not assume axis order without checking the object record.
- Christie's Ethiopian wooden chair lot records a three-curved-leg seat and pierced triangular backrest; height 35½ in (90.2 cm). This is an auction description, not a current retail listing.

The requested minimum of ten stool listings should be populated from Zebra's actual stool inventory and individually measured. Do not fill ten slots with Tonga/Asante items from other countries or duplicate the same dimensions.

## Comparable selling and catalogue models

1. Hemingway African Gallery — specialist online store, individual SKUs, item-specific photos, dimensions, materials, origin, shipping quote and enquiry/contact details. Best benchmark for product-page structure and direct-to-customer retail presentation.
2. Sotheby's Buy Now / auction catalogue — condition grades, period, materials, country of origin, dimensions, offer/auction model. Useful for collector-grade object records and condition disclosure.
3. Christie's auction catalogue — concise object description, dimensions, provenance/lot essay and specialist auction context. Useful for careful attribution and historical context.
4. Harvard Peabody Museum collections — object number, object description, materials, date, geography, dimensions and provenance. Useful for checking terminology and documenting Ethiopian forms.
5. Smithsonian National Museum of African Art — dimensions, materials, maker/cultural context and interpretive label. Useful for accurate stool and furniture descriptions.

## Website implementation requirements
- Keep the dark charcoal / warm ivory / muted gold visual identity, but use a distinct Zebra Gallery layout and copy.
- Add category navigation for Ethiopian chairs, stools, tables, traditional furniture, masks and sculpture, religious art, crosses, and souvenirs.
